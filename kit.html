Before you leave the car

Ask for the record, then the proof.

No single US law covers every body repair. These four checks do. Ask to see each one before the keys change hands.

  • Written authorization. California Business and Professions Code 9884.9, current in 2025, says no work starts and no charge accrues until you authorize it. A collision estimate must list parts and labor, and say if a part is new, used, rebuilt, or reconditioned. Nothing above that estimate is charged without a new written authorization.
  • Warranty rights. The Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act, 15 U.S.C. 2302(c), bars a maker from tying coverage to its dealer or its parts unless that part or service is free. The FTC says a claim can be refused only if the maker proves the independent repair, or the other part, caused that failure.
  • Credentials you can verify. I-CAR Gold Class is earned, not bought. The estimator, refinish, structural, and non-structural roles must be at ProLevel 2 or higher, with annual training. For 2026 renewals, a shop that calibrates driver-assist systems in house must list an ADAS technician. ASE is the technician's test. An automaker certification means that brand approved the procedures and the equipment.
  • Workmanship, in writing. Peeling or cracking on panels we sprayed is covered. So is a weld or frame pull we did, if that repair fails. A new impact, rust already in the car, and a part you told us not to replace are not. Call with the invoice number. No new estimate for our own work.

On the road

The record travels with the car.

A repair that followed the maker's procedure is a document a later buyer can read. The market around that car is measured once a year, not live.

  • PROCEDURE Scan, repair, and calibrate to the procedure for that vehicle.
  • RECORD The authorization and the calibration certificate stay with the car.
  • RESALE A documented repair is not priced like one without the paperwork.
